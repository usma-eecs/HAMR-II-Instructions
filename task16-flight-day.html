Task 16: Flight day

Complete the HAMR II preflight check, plan the ATAK mission, review emergency actions, and report to the lane OIC for final release.

Parts required

  • Completed HAMR II vehicle
  • 4S flight battery

Tools required

  • Faculty or lane OIC verification
  • Ground control station
  • ATAK device
  • Xbox controller
  • Battery checker
Task 16 is a flight-day preparation gate.
Do not rebuild the vehicle here. Complete the preflight check, plan the ATAK mission, review emergency actions, and get lane OIC release before any prop-on operation or flight.

Step 1. Complete the HAMR II preflight check

Mark each item before reporting to the lane OIC. Any Fix Needed item must be corrected before flight.

CheckPassFix Needed
All four FC/ESC stack locknuts are tight. Four 8 mm spacers are installed between the flight controller and ESC, both boards are flat, and the silicone rubber dampers are not crushed.
FC/ESC stack does not move when gently checked.
Motors are fastened and do not wobble.
Camera/GPS superstructure is fastened and does not wobble.
XT60 ring terminals are secure, red is on ESC positive, black is on ESC negative, and the terminals and leads are clear of the FC/ESC cable, ESC connector, stack, and mounting hardware.
XT60 battery lead is secured at the rear so only the connector extends beyond the rear edge.
Motor wires, GPS cable, FC/RPi cable, camera ribbon, and battery lead are restrained and clear of all propeller arcs.
Propellers match the verified motor spin directions, label sides face up, locknuts are snug, and no propeller wobbles.
Battery strap passes through the upper-deck slots and is flat, centered, and accessible from above.
Camera lens is clear of film, tape, fingerprints, and other obstruction.
Flight battery voltage is at least 15.0 V before flight.

Step 2. Initialize UAS Tool and build the mission

Use the current ATAK UAS Tool workflow to connect to the aircraft, create the mission, and upload it before reporting for final release. Do not arm the vehicle until directed.

Connect to the aircraft
  1. Insert the issued microSD card into the Raspberry Pi. Place the vehicle stationary and level in the launch area, then connect the flight battery.
  2. Connect the ATAK device to the vehicle network.
  3. Launch ATAK, then launch UAS Tool.
  4. Wait for the aircraft to be detected and for parameter loading to complete. Allow up to 2 minutes.
  5. Press the aircraft Play button to open the aircraft interface.
  6. Verify that the video feed is active and that the displayed battery voltage is at least 15.0 V.
  7. Turn on the Xbox controller, verify that its power light is constantly on, and use the D-pad to check the camera’s full tilt travel.
Create the task
  1. Open the UAS Tool Menu and select Missions.
  2. Press Add Task (+), then select Create New Task in the popup window.
  3. Set Initial Waypoint Alt as appropriate while maintaining terrain and obstacle clearance. Do not exceed 400 ft AGL.
  4. Check Set Flight Speed.
  5. Set Flight Speed to 9 mph, or as directed.
  6. Press Add Mission Item (+), select TAKEOFF, then select ADD.
  7. Add each required WAYPOINT in the same manner and select its location on the map.
  8. Add an optional Return to Launch mission item at the end when directed. Without RTL, the aircraft loiters at the last waypoint.
  9. Select Save.
Upload and verify the task
  1. Review the complete task route.
  2. If a previously uploaded, yellow-bordered task already exists, open that task’s More Options menu and select Clear.
  3. Open the new task’s More Options menu and select Upload.
  4. Verify that the task has a yellow border and is listed under Onboard.
If no aircraft is detected after 2 minutes.
Force close ATAK using Recent Apps, relaunch ATAK and UAS Tool, and wait for parameter loading. If the aircraft is still not detected, power cycle the vehicle and notify the lane OIC if the problem continues.
If the battery is displayed as a percentage.
Press and hold the battery symbol for 3 seconds, press Back to return to Network UAS, then press the aircraft Play button again.
If no video feed is present.
Press the camera icon. If the feed does not appear, restart ATAK and UAS Tool, then power cycle the vehicle if required. Do not launch until the video feed and camera control are verified.
Mission review standard.
The task route is reviewed, the uploaded task has a yellow border and appears under Onboard, and the route remains clear of personnel, buildings, trees, vehicles, and other obstructions.

Step 3. Review emergency procedures

Review these actions after mission planning and before reporting to the lane OIC. Protect people first, then regain or terminate control as the situation requires.

ConditionPrimary action
Unexpected aircraft behaviorCheck battery level and flight mode. If a battery-failsafe notification is displayed, clear the return and landing area and monitor the aircraft. Otherwise press B to select LOITER and take manual control.
Aircraft does not respond to Xbox controlVerify that the Xbox controller is on and its power light is constantly on. Select RTL or LAND from the ATAK device.
Aircraft on a collision coursePress B immediately, even if RTL or LAND is active. In LOITER, manually fly clear of the hazard, then select RTL.
Aircraft departing the assigned area or collision imminentDirect personnel to take cover or clear the area. Visually clear the area below the aircraft if practicable. Press ARM/DISARM, select Force, verify that the propellers stop, and advise the lane OIC.
Force-disarm limitation.
Force disarm only when continued powered flight presents the greater hazard. Do not approach the vehicle until all propellers have stopped.

Common errors

  • Leaving an FC/ESC stack locknut loose, omitting a spacer, crushing a damper, or allowing a circuit board to bow.
  • Leaving motor wires, camera ribbon, GPS cable, FC/RPi cable, or battery lead where a propeller can strike it.
  • Flying with loose motors, loose superstructure, or loose propeller nuts.
  • Leaving the XT60 battery lead unsecured or with excess cable hanging aft.
  • Uploading a task without confirming the yellow border and Onboard status.
  • Planning a task that passes near obstructions or outside the assigned lane controls.
