Task 16: Flight Day

Review emergency actions, complete a short HAMR II preflight check, plan the ATAK mission, and report to the lane OIC for final release.

Parts required

  • Completed HAMR II aircraft
  • 4S flight battery

Tools required

  • Faculty or lane OIC verification
  • Ground-station computer
  • ATAK device
  • Battery checker
Task 16 is a flight-day preparation gate.
Do not rebuild the aircraft here. Confirm the safety-critical end states below, plan the mission in ATAK, and get lane OIC release before any prop-on operation or flight.

Step 1. Review emergency procedures

Review these actions before the aircraft is powered. When in doubt, aviate, navigate, then communicate.

Emergency priority.
Protect people first. Do not approach the aircraft until all propellers have stopped.

Step 2. Complete the HAMR II preflight check

Focus on the errors most likely to create a safety problem on HAMR II.

  1. FC/ESC stack lower lock nuts: about two full threads protrude below each lock nut. The stack is not loose, and the dampers are not crushed.
  2. Motors and superstructure: all motors are fastened with no wobble. Frame and Camera/GPS mount hardware are snug with no loose standoffs.
  3. XT60 battery lead: red is on ESC positive, black is on ESC negative, terminal screws are snug, and the ring terminals do not touch or block the underside JST-SH connector.
  4. XT60 strain relief: the battery lead is tied to the lower deck so only the yellow XT60 connector extends beyond the rear edge.
  5. Battery restraint: the battery pad slots are open. The strap passes through the upper-deck slots and is flat, centered, and accessible.
  6. Wiring clearance: motor wires, GPS cable, TELEM cable, camera ribbon, and battery lead are restrained. No wiring enters any propeller arc.
  7. Propellers: propellers match the verified motor spin directions, label sides face up, lock nuts are snug, and no propeller has visible wobble or play.
  8. Camera and sensor suite: camera lens is clear of film or obstruction. GPS/compass and camera mount hardware are secure.
  9. Flight battery: battery voltage is at least 15.0 V before flight.
FC/ESC stack check. About two full turns should protrude below each lower lock nut.
Battery lead check. Secure the XT60 lead so only the connector extends beyond the rear edge.

Step 3. Plan the ATAK mission

Use ATAK UAS Tool to create and review the mission before reporting for final release. Do not arm the aircraft until directed.

Device setup
  1. Connect the ATAK device to the GCS using USB-C.
  2. Launch ATAK.
  3. Open the Tools menu and select UAS Tool.
  4. From the Flight menu, select the play button to load the UAS interface.
Mission setup
  1. Swipe left from the Flight menu to open the Task menu.
  2. Select Add New Task.
  3. Set altitude at least 150 ft AGL unless clearance is assured. Do not exceed 400 ft AGL.
  4. Set flight speed no higher than 10 m/s. Recommended speed is 7 m/s.
  5. Add TAKEOFF, WAYPOINT, and LAND mission items.
  6. Place waypoints and landing location well clear of obstructions.
  7. Select Save, then swipe right to return to the Flight menu.
Queueing and troubleshooting
  1. Queue the mission only when directed.
  2. If the mission fails to queue, disable ATAK device Wi-Fi and try again.
  3. If needed, restart ATAK and UAS Tool.
  4. If still unsuccessful, notify the lane OIC before power cycling the vehicle.

Step 4. Report to the lane OIC

On flight day, report to the lane OIC to receive communications equipment and final inspection. If reassigned to a lane that does not match your current Team ID, reset the SIP switches as directed.

Common errors

  • Overtightening or leaving loose the FC/ESC stack lower lock nuts.
  • Leaving motor wires, camera ribbon, GPS cable, TELEM cable, or battery lead where a propeller can strike it.
  • Flying with loose motors, loose superstructure, or loose propeller nuts.
  • Leaving the XT60 battery lead unsecured or with excess cable hanging aft.
  • Planning an ATAK mission that passes near obstructions or outside the assigned lane controls.
