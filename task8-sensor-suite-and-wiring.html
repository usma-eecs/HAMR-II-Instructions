Parts required

  • Vehicle with base plate and Raspberry Pi installed
  • Completed sensor-suite superstructure
  • 6-wire GPS cable
  • Custom FC-to-RPi harness
  • Four M3 × 10 mm socket-head screws

Tools required

  • 2.5 mm Allen key

Steps

  1. Open the CSI camera connector on the Raspberry Pi gently by lifting the black retainer.
  2. Insert the camera ribbon cable fully and evenly into the Raspberry Pi CSI connector. On the Raspberry Pi end, the ribbon cable metal contacts must face away from the black retainer.
  3. Close the CSI retainer and lightly tug the cable to confirm it is locked.
  4. Place the completed sensor-suite superstructure onto the base plate with the correct front-facing orientation.
  5. Align the four standoffs with the four mounting holes in the base plate and install the four M3 × 10 mm socket-head screws from underneath. Start all four before tightening.
  6. Tighten the four base-to-superstructure screws in a crisscross pattern with the 2.5 mm Allen key.
  7. Route the 6-wire GPS cable cleanly to the flight controller and connect it to the FC port labeled GPS.
  8. Connect the custom FC-to-RPi harness between the Raspberry Pi latch header from Task 1 and the FC port labeled TELEM.
  9. Route cables so they do not get pinched between plates and do not hang into the propeller arc. Add cable ties near the main body as needed.
  10. Install the battery strap around the center of the top plate.
STOP! Ask for a faculty member to verify the camera ribbon insertion, the GPS connection to the FC port labeled GPS, and the custom harness connection to the FC port labeled TELEM before you proceed to software setup.

Common errors

  • Inserting the camera ribbon cable backwards. The metal contacts face away from the black retainer on both ends.
  • Not seating the ribbon cable fully before closing the retainer.
  • Rotating the superstructure and forcing the standoffs to fit.
  • Routing GPS or TELEM cables into the wrong FC ports.
  • Pinching the camera ribbon or GPS cable between the superstructure and base plate.
  • Leaving loose motor or harness wiring where propellers will later pass.
